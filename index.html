The path forward

Commit in stages. Resolve the big unknowns first.

01

Before an offer is binding

Confirm measured area. Inspect the foundation, basement moisture, electrical, plumbing, roof and the septic system. Get local agent feedback on resale range.

02

Before renovation spending

Written quotes for the porch, courtyard, kitchen, bath, half bath and flooring. Check permits and any Village of Milford rules for the porch addition. Lock a scope that fits the budget.

03

First year

Essential repairs first, then the half bath, kitchen, bath, flooring and finishes, with the porch and courtyard last. Keep permits, receipts and before/after records.

04

At resale

Refresh comps, recalculate selling costs, then set the list price.

Verify before committing
  • Age of the house: built in 1901. Check for knob-and-tube wiring, lead paint, and foundation and basement moisture before committing to the budget.
  • Septic: the listing shows a septic sewer. Get a septic inspection, and confirm the system can take a second bathroom.
  • Half bath: the listing says the laundry/mudroom is big enough for a half or full bath. Plumbing, venting and permits still need a plumber's review.
  • Porch and courtyard: the porch extension and outdoor kitchen need permits, setback checks on the corner lot, and real quotes. They are the easiest items to scale back if the budget runs tight.
  • Draft numbers: offer, renovation budget and resale price are placeholders. Replace them before relying on the profit figure.
  • Margin is thin at the working case: about $14K at $425K resale, and a loss at $390K. Cost control is the project.
